How the dice actually work

You pick how honest the die is. Fair is honest. The other two are not, on purpose.

Look at the probability engine while you roll. The bars are the live chances.

Fair

This is the boring one. Every face starts even and stays even. A d20 is 5%. A d6 is 16.667%. Last roll does not change the next one. Reset weights does nothing useful here. There is nothing to reset.

Use Fair for normal, multi session games where the chances will eventually come around.

Fairish

This is the default. Duh.

After a face lands, that face loses a chunk of chance. The chunk is a share of the even starting chance, not a share of whatever the face currently has. Drop strength is that share. At 20% on a d20, a 5% face loses 1 percentage point.

That mass does not vanish. It moves to the opposite half of the die. On a d20, 1 through 10 are low and 11 through 20 are high. Roll a 17 and the high faces keep their odds. Each low face gains a slice of the drop. Faces in the same half as the roll are left alone. A face never goes below 0%. Once it is spent, rolling it again does not move anything until you hit Reset weights.

A worked d20 at 20% drop:

  1. Start. Every face is 5%.
  2. You roll 20. Face 20 drops to 4%. Faces 1 through 10 each pick up 0.1%. Faces 11 through 19 stay at 5%.
  3. The next roll is more likely to be low. If a low face lands, probability flows back toward the high half.

Drop strength lives in Settings. 0% means Fairish still tracks faces but the drop is zero, so odds stay even until you raise it. 100% tries to dump the entire base chance off the face in one hit.

Use this for short sessions where few rolls are expected and the averages are very hard to achieve. This dice mode gives you the chance to not suck all the time.

Average

This one is for DMs and designers.

Each reported face is the rounded mean of N independent fair rolls. Curve rolls is N. Default is 2. Two fair d20s that come up 3 and 18 report 11. A 1 only happens when the component rolls are all 1, or close enough that rounding still lands there. Raise curve rolls and the bell tightens around the middle.

The probability bars do not move after you roll. They already are the curve. Find out if your trap or breath weapon is more or less likely to kill the party, or if that DC you eyeballed is a joke.

Aggregation

Aggregation is a fancy word for combining dice. Leave it on None and you get one face per result, like a normal die. Number of rolls is how many of those results you get from one click.

Pick Advantage, Disadvantage, or Sum and Dice per roll shows up. Number of rolls is how many combinations you get. Dice per roll is how many dice are used in each aggregation. Advantage keeps the highest. Disadvantage keeps the lowest. Sum adds them.

Three attacks with advantage. Number of rolls 3, Aggregation Advantage, Dice per roll 2. One click, three numbers, each is the higher of two dice.

An 8d6 fireball. Number of rolls 1, Aggregation Sum, Dice per roll 8, die set to d6.

Fairish still remembers faces. With Sum, every face that came up loses chance. With Advantage or Disadvantage, only the face you keep loses chance. The die you threw away does not change the memory.

Probability engine

Visualy see how the math maths.

These are the bars. Single die is how likely each face is on the die you picked, right now. In Fair every bar is the same height. In Fairish the heights change after every roll. In Average they make a hill in the middle and stay there.

Turn aggregation on and a second set of bars shows up. That one is the number you write down, not the individual dice. 2d6 Sum goes from 2 to 12, and 7 is the tall bar. Advantage on a d20 is still 1 through 20, but the high faces get fatter.

Those bars use the odds as they are this second. After a Fairish roll the odds change, so look again if you want the new picture. Reset weights is in here too. That wipes Fairish memory on the selected die.

What Default settings does not touch

Default settings restores the tray and the two knobs, drop strength and curve rolls. History stays. Face weights stay. If you want a clean Fairish memory, hit Reset weights in the probability engine.
